# From impounded pool to river and floodplain: the post-breach transformation of the Kakhovka Reservoir from harmonised satellite altimetry, optical and radar imagery, legacy hydrography and vegetation succession

## ABSTRACT

**Background.** The destruction of the Kakhovka Dam on 6 June 2023 drained a 2 155 km² reservoir on the lower Dnipro within days. Whether the residual system now behaves as a river, and what surface it flows over, are questions that a single water-area time series cannot answer: they require water-surface geometry, a bed, and the roughness of what has grown on it, all in one vertical frame.

**Methods.** Seven hydrological gauges, ICESat-2 ATL13 and ATL08 laser altimetry, SWOT PIXC/RiverSP radar interferometry, Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 imagery and a legacy navigation-chart survey are harmonised into EVRF2019 normal heights through the EGG2015 quasigeoid and the EPSG:9902 operation, with the permanent-tide convention of each product handled explicitly. Per-overpass longitudinal water-surface slopes are estimated with a robust (Theil–Sen) fit, one overpass being the independent unit. The former water surface is classified on leaf-on Sentinel-2 dates into ten surface classes, woody cover is followed with Dynamic World probabilities, channel width below the dam is measured on 157 transects at matched downstream stage, and class areas are translated into Manning roughness ranges from published priors.

Within-overpass water-surface heterogeneity rose from {{claim:M2.1}}. A soundings-only bed reconstruction reproduces the historical drawdown exposure ({{claim:V10.1}}) and, in the downstream zones, soundings-only interpolation outperforms every shoreline-constrained variant ({{claim:B2.3}}). On the former water surface the vegetated share rose to {{claim:S1.1}} after {{claim:S1.1a}}, Dynamic World woody probability reached {{claim:W1.1}}, and the translated area-weighted Manning roughness moved from {{claim:N1.1}}. Below the dam the wetted width on comparable dates is {{claim:K1.1}} against {{claim:K1.1a}}.

**Conclusions.** The reservoir did not merely shrink: its water surface acquired a persistent downstream gradient, its bed is now a vegetating floodplain with roughly twice the roughness it had as open water, and the channel below the dam is wider. Each of these statements rests on a measured quantity with a stated uncertainty and, where a quantity cannot be measured with the data at hand — canopy-height change, a post-breach slope–discharge law, classification accuracy — the manuscript says so rather than inferring it.
