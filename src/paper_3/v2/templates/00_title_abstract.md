# From impounded pool to river: water-surface geometry and post-breach hydraulic transformation of the former Kakhovka Reservoir

## ABSTRACT

**Background.** The destruction of the Kakhovka Dam on 6 June 2023 drained a 2 155 km² reservoir on the lower Dnipro within days. Whether the residual system now behaves as a river, over what bed it flows, and how the surface it exposed changes the resistance to flow are questions a water-area time series cannot answer. They require water-surface geometry, a bed and a surface description that are commensurable in one vertical frame.

**Methods.** Seven hydrological gauges, ICESat-2 ATL13 laser altimetry, SWOT PIXC/RiverSP radar interferometry and a legacy navigation-chart survey are harmonised into EVRF2019 normal heights through the EGG2015 quasigeoid and the EPSG:9902 operation, with the permanent-tide convention of each product handled explicitly. Per-overpass longitudinal water-surface slopes are estimated with a robust (Theil–Sen) fit, one overpass being the independent unit. The bed is reconstructed from soundings only under spatially blocked cross-validation and tested against the historical drawdown-exposure statistic. Surface classes mapped on leaf-on Sentinel-2 dates, with Dynamic World woody probabilities, are translated into a hydraulically interpreted roughness layer through published priors; channel width below the dam is measured on 157 transects at matched downstream stage.

**Results.** Within the former reservoir footprint the per-overpass slope changed from {{claim:M1.1}} (95 % CI {{claim:M1.1.unc}}), and within-overpass water-surface heterogeneity rose from {{claim:M2.1}}. The soundings-only bed reproduces the historical drawdown exposure ({{claim:V10.1}}), and in the downstream zones soundings-only interpolation outperforms every shoreline-constrained variant ({{claim:B2.3}}). On the former water surface the vegetated share rose to {{claim:S1.1}} after {{claim:S1.1a}}; translated through literature priors, the area-weighted roughness of the surface moved from {{claim:N1.1}} — a derived, not calibrated, quantity. Below the dam the wetted width at matched stage is {{claim:K1.1}} against {{claim:K1.1a}}.

**Conclusions.** The reservoir did not merely shrink: its water surface acquired a persistent downstream gradient, its bed is now a vegetating floodplain whose mapped surface implies roughly twice the flow resistance of open water, and the channel below the dam is wider. Each statement rests on a measured quantity with a stated uncertainty; where a quantity cannot be measured with the data at hand — a post-breach slope–discharge law, canopy-height change, classification accuracy, calibrated roughness — the manuscript says so rather than inferring it.
