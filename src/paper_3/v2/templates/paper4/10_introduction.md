# 1. INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 A new floodplain where a reservoir was

A drained reservoir bed is also a new floodplain. The companion paper showed that after the breach the water surface within the former Kakhovka Reservoir footprint acquired a persistent downstream gradient and reconstructed the bed it now flows over. Between that bed and the water is a surface that changed from open water to bare sediment to reed, herbaceous cover and young willow within two seasons.

## 1.2 What the field surveys established and what they leave open

Field surveys in the first seasons after the breach documented rapid colonisation dominated by willow, with seedling densities of tens of individuals per square metre and heights approaching the shrub–tree boundary within a year ({{cite:kuzemko2025}}; {{cite:didukh2024}}). They provide a habitat map and the botanical record. What they do not provide is a multi-year, whole-surface area and transition series on one grid, or a translation of the emerging cover into the hydraulic resistance that any reconstruction of the breach flood, or of future floods, must assume.

## 1.3 Roughness, conveyance and the two hydraulic states

Manning roughness for vegetated flood plains has a long literature of priors ({{cite:arcement1989}}; {{cite:jarvela2004}}), and translating a class map into a roughness field is standard in two-dimensional modelling. What is not standard is doing so on a surface that is changing year by year, and then asking how the current channel-and-floodplain configuration conveys a given discharge compared with the impounded pool it replaced.

## 1.4 Objectives

(i) Quantify the surface transformation of the former water surface over 2023–2025 as class areas, transitions and first-event timing, and woody encroachment. (ii) Translate it into a roughness layer with stated priors and uncertainty. (iii) Model the pre-breach and current systems in HEC-RAS on the companion paper's terrain, calibrating only on legacy backwater profiles and gauge records. (iv) Validate the current model against the independently observed post-breach longitudinal water-surface geometry and channel width. (v) Bound the stage and conveyance response to roughness uncertainty. Where the data cannot yet answer — post-breach discharge, classification accuracy, canopy growth — the paper states the limitation instead of inferring an answer.
