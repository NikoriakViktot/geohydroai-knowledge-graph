# From exposed bed to floodplain: vegetation succession, surface roughness and the hydraulic consequences of the Kakhovka Dam breach

## ABSTRACT

**Background.** The breach of the Kakhovka Dam on 6 June 2023 turned a 2 155 km² impounded pool into a river with a vegetating floodplain. A companion paper established the new water-surface geometry and reconstructed the bed in one vertical frame; what that geometry and the surface growing on it mean for flow resistance, conveyance and stage is the question here.

**Methods.** On the former water surface ({{claim:S1.5}}) ten surface classes are mapped on every leaf-on Sentinel-2 date of 2023–2025, woody cover is followed with Dynamic World probabilities, and class maps are translated state by state into Manning roughness ranges from published priors. Channel width below the dam is measured on 157 transects at matched downstream stage. One-dimensional and two-dimensional HEC-RAS models of the pre-breach and current systems use the companion paper's bed as terrain; roughness is calibrated only on legacy backwater profiles and gauge records, and the models are validated against the independently observed post-breach longitudinal water-surface geometry.
