# 6. DISCUSSION

## 6.1 Novelty boundary

Field-based habitat maps of the same bed exist ({{cite:kuzemko2025}}; {{cite:didukh2024}}); the contribution here is not the classification but the multi-year, whole-surface area and transition series on one grid, its translation into a roughness layer, and the hydraulic consequence of that layer once the models exist.

## 6.2 Succession as a hydraulic driver

Two seasons turned most of the former water surface into reed, herbaceous cover and young willow; translated through published priors, the mapped surface implies roughly twice the area-weighted flow resistance of the open pool ({{claim:N1.1}}). The floodplain forest documented in the field ({{cite:didukh2024}}) is the class whose prior is widest ({{claim:N1.4a}}). {{pending:OPEN67|Discussion of modelled stage/conveyance change and of calibrated versus prior roughness|backs=P2-AT02,P2-AT03|section=6.2|blocks_submission=yes}}

## 6.3 Why calibration and validation are kept apart

If the roughness were tuned to the satellite profiles and then said to reproduce them, nothing would have been tested. Calibration therefore uses only the legacy backwater profiles and the gauges, and the observed post-breach slope of the companion paper is met, or not, as an independent target.

## 6.4 The channel below the dam

A wider wetted surface at matched stage, transient braiding in the weeks after the breach, and bank displacement below the detection limit describe an adjusting reach; the modelled wetted width is the test of whether the two-dimensional model captures it. The comparison with dam-removal reaches elsewhere is withheld until the literature retrieval has been validated.

## 6.6 Limitations

The surface classes carry no accuracy assessment until the 320-point stratified sample is labelled. Canopy-height change is not identifiable ({{claim:H1.2}}). Dynamic World composites for 2025–2026 are missing. Post-breach discharge has not been recovered, so the current-state runs are scenario runs (companion paper, C-14). The roughness priors are literature values with no local hydraulic calibration until Section 5.5; the DniproGES1D legacy model is used for comparison only. The SAR water masks and the classifier variants tested and rejected are described in Supplementary Methods S2 and play no part in the argument.
